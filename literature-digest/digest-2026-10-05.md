## A. Spatial proteomics / CODEX-type imaging

### 1. Niche-level immune evasion in TP53-mutant AML residual disease revealed by spatial proteomics (bioRxiv preprint, May 2026)
PMID 42239259 · DOI 10.64898/2026.05.15.725421
**Summary:** Sequential immunofluorescence (multiplexed imaging) of whole bone marrow biopsies from TP53-wild-type and TP53-mutant AML at diagnosis and in morphological complete remission (CR), mapping hematopoietic, stromal and immune niches. Mutant cells were identified by high p53 expression. Closest paper to your project this round (spatial BM map, AML, outcome-relevant MRD).
**Main findings:**
- TP53-mutant BM at CR: marked B-cell depletion and increased regulatory T cells.
- Persistent erythroid and immature leukemia cell clusters in perisinusoidal niches, spatially segregated from T-cell clusters (niche-level immune evasion).
- FOXP3-high Tregs enriched near mutant MRD cells (local immunosuppression).
- scRNA-seq cell-cell communication: erythroid–T-cell GDF15–CD48 axis as a candidate T-cell suppression mechanism.
- Spatial proteomics can identify actionable MRD niches.

### 2. IN-DEPTH: same-slide spatial multiomics (Cancer Discovery, Aug 2026)
PMID 41874448 · DOI 10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-0775
**Summary:** Method paper. Single-cell spatial proteomics imaging guides spatial transcriptomic capture on the same slide, without RNA loss, plus Spectral Graph Cross-Correlation (SGCC) to integrate protein and RNA layers. Applied to EBV+ vs EBV- DLBCL.
**Main findings:**
- Protein-first workflow preserves epitopes, RNA quality and tissue integrity; compatible with commercial platforms.
- Revealed coordinated tumor–macrophage–CD4 T-cell remodeling, C1Q+ immunosuppressive macrophages, CD4 T-cell dysfunction.
- Candidate IL27–STAT3 axis. Relevant if you want to add transcriptomics to CODEX data.

### 3. EBV+ DLBCL: loss of antiviral immunity and LMP1-driven suppressive myeloid niches (Leukemia, 2026)
PMID 42271039 · DOI 10.1038/s41375-026-02994-3
**Summary:** Combines functional profiling of peripheral antiviral T cells with high-dimensional spatial proteomics of tumors.
**Main findings:**
- Broad antiviral T-cell impairment in EBV+ and EBV- DLBCL patients vs controls, worst in EBV+.
- EBV+ tumors: fewer intratumoral CD8+ T cells, expanded PD-1+ Tregs/exhausted T cells, dense PD-L1+/IDO1+ macrophage aggregates.
- Suppressive myeloid niches concentrate around LMP1-expressing tumor cells (not seen in EBV+ cHL or infectious mononucleosis).

## B. Pediatric AML / MDS / JMML

### 4. Single-cell chromatin profiling reveals relapse-related priming in pediatric AML (Life Science Alliance, Aug 2026)
PMID 42637517 · DOI 10.26508/lsa.202603746
**Summary:** scATAC-seq (177,500 cells, 16 diagnostic pAML samples: t(8;21), inv(16), FLT3-ITD) linked to long-term outcome; relapse vs non-relapse.
**Main findings:**
- Relapse-associated chromatin signatures are detectable at diagnosis across subtypes.
- Heightened innate immune/inflammatory activation; expansion of HSC/MPP-like leukemic progenitors.
- AP-1, RUNX1, SPI1, ETS motifs central; high expression predicts inferior survival in independent cohorts.

### 5. ROS levels define leukemia stem cell identity in pediatric AML (Cancer Letters, Oct 2026)
PMID 42830144 · DOI 10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218866
**Summary:** Sorted ROS-low vs ROS-high pediatric AML cells and tested stemness, niche behavior and metabolism.
**Main findings:**
- ROS-low cells: quiescent, serial colony formation, mouse engraftment, cytarabine-resistant, localize to hypoxic regions in a 3D niche model.
- Enriched stemness/adhesion/niche-interaction programs; low OXPHOS yet paradoxical dependency on complex I.
- ROS-low signature identifies LSCs in scRNA-seq regardless of differentiation state and is prognostic.

### 6. IL-17A/PTGS2/NLRP3 targeting in juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia (Blood, Sep 2026)
PMID 42233403 · DOI 10.1182/blood.2025032588
**Summary:** Shp2 E76K/+ (PTPN11) JMML mouse model, patient samples and PDX; pediatric MDS/MPN overlap, with murine functional validation like your aim 3.
**Main findings:**
- Mutant macrophages upregulate IL-17A, driving NLRP3 inflammasome, PTGS2, IL-1β and marrow inflammation.
- Treg expansion, T-cell exhaustion, impaired cytotoxicity.
- IL-17A neutralization or dual NLRP3/PTGS2 inhibition (+ MEK inhibition) restored T-cell function, reversed myeloproliferation and prolonged survival in mice and PDX.

## C. Bone marrow niche in AML (adult/mouse)

### 7. FGF18 mediates fibroblast–leukemia crosstalk in AML (Blood, Oct 2026)
PMID 42360909 · DOI 10.1182/blood.2026033125
**Summary:** scRNA-seq of the AML marrow niche, fibroblast-specific KO, CRISPR screen, therapeutic antibody in mice.
**Main findings:**
- Fgf18 upregulated in stromal fibroblasts; FGF18 accelerates AML, fibroblast Fgf18 depletion delays it.
- FGF18→FGFR3→AKT–mTOR→IL-6; IL-6 activates fibroblast JAK–STAT3, creating a feed-forward loop that suppresses CD8+ T cells.
- High FGF18 correlates with poor prognosis; neutralizing antibody restores CD8 function and synergizes with anti-PD-1.

### 8. BM mesenchymal stem cells are functionally remodeled after AML complete remission (J Transl Intern Med, Aug 2026)
PMID 42657369 · DOI 10.1515/jtim-2026-0048
**Summary:** MSCs from de novo AML, AML-CR and healthy donors: function, senescence, RNA-seq; AML-CR mouse model.
**Main findings:**
- Dysfunctional AML MSCs recover in CR (differentiation, apoptosis, cytokines), approaching healthy MSC transcriptome.
- Fewer senescent MSCs after CR, but they can be re-induced to senescence by AML cells.
- Suggests the stroma can be remodeled to support normal HSCs after remission.
